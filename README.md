These are the books, reports and papers we recommend to people who work on illicit trade: analysts, investigators, journalists, students at the Summer School on Illicit Trade, and colleagues who ask us where to start. Every entry carries a full citation, an ISBN or DOI, and a few sentences on why it is worth your time and who it suits.

The list is short on purpose and it is not exhaustive. If a title you rely on is missing, please [propose it](#contributing). We reply to every proposal.

## How to read an entry

Each entry gives the title (linked to the edition we recommend on Open Library, or to the DOI), a citation in Chicago author-date style with the ISBN-13 or DOI, then **Why read it** and **Who it is for**. A note at the end marks titles that are open access, self-published, or out of print and hard to find. Where we cite a reissue or translation, the first year in brackets is the original.

## How this list is maintained

The list lives in one data file, `data/library.yml`. A script builds this page, the website and the downloads from it, and checks every ISBN against Open Library and every DOI against Crossref or DataCite once a month. We sweep the whole list twice a year with our other lists, re-check editions, and record changes in the [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md). Each sweep ends with a GitHub release.

## Financial crime and illicit networks

How dirty money moves, who moves it and why the system lets them. Start with the one marked first; it is the shortest route into the subject.

- <a id="findley2014"></a>**[Global Shell Games: Experiments in Transnational Relations, Crime, and Terrorism](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781107337848)**<br>
  Findley, Michael G., Daniel L. Nielson, and J. C. Sharman. 2014. *Global Shell Games: Experiments in Transnational Relations, Crime, and Terrorism*. Cambridge University Press. ISBN 9781107638839. <https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781107337848>.<br>
  **Why read it:** An experimental study of how easy it is to set up an anonymous shell company. The authors posed as consultants and approached nearly 4,000 corporate service providers in more than 180 countries, then recorded who asked for identity documents and who did not. The results still frame how we think about the gaps that let terrorist financing, corruption and arms trading through. **Who it is for:** analysts new to beneficial ownership and shell companies.
- <a id="shelley2020"></a>**[Dark Commerce: How a New Illicit Economy Is Threatening Our Future](https://doi.org/10.1515/9780691184296)**<br>
  Shelley, Louise I. (2018) 2020. *Dark Commerce: How a New Illicit Economy Is Threatening Our Future*. Princeton University Press. ISBN 9780691209760. <https://doi.org/10.1515/9780691184296>.<br>
  **Why read it:** An account of how technology changed illicit trade, from drugs and people to wildlife and cybercrime. Shelley shows how these markets connect to each other and how digital anonymity and globalisation let them scale, which is the background every illicit trade analyst needs. **Who it is for:** anyone who needs the big picture of illicit trade in one book.
- <a id="wittig2011"></a>**[Understanding Terrorist Finance](https://doi.org/10.1057/9780230316935)**<br>
  Wittig, Timothy. 2011. *Understanding Terrorist Finance*. Palgrave Macmillan. ISBN 9781349332120. <https://doi.org/10.1057/9780230316935>.<br>
  **Why read it:** A framework for analysing terrorist finance that dispels the popular myths about how groups get and use money. Wittig shows that terrorists work through everyday economic systems rather than a separate shadow finance world, which changes where an analyst should look. Priced for libraries, so borrow it or read it through a university subscription. **Who it is for:** counter-terrorism finance analysts.

*Last checked 2026-10-13.*

## Global economics and politics

The wider economy that illicit trade lives inside: offshore finance, informal markets and states that work differently from the textbook.

- <a id="shaxson2012"></a>**[Treasure Islands: Tax Havens and the Men Who Stole the World](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9780099541721)**<br>
  Shaxson, Nicholas. (2011) 2012. *Treasure Islands: Tax Havens and the Men Who Stole the World*. Vintage. ISBN 9780099541721.<br>
  **Why read it:** A readable account of the offshore system: what tax havens are, how they grew and who benefits. It explains the plumbing that most illicit trade investigations eventually run into. **Who it is for:** readers new to offshore finance.
- <a id="little2003"></a>**[Somalia: Economy without State](https://doi.org/10.2979/3009.0)**<br>
  Little, Peter D. 2003. *Somalia: Economy without State*. Indiana University Press. ISBN 9780253216489. <https://doi.org/10.2979/3009.0>. Out of print or hard to find.<br>
  **Why read it:** How Somalia's economy kept working, and in places grew, without a central government: livestock exports, remittances and trade networks that run on trust and clan. It challenges the assumption that markets need a state, which matters for anyone working on ungoverned spaces. **Who it is for:** anyone working on the Horn of Africa, or on trade where there is no state.
- <a id="nordstrom2007"></a>**[Global Outlaws: Crime, Money, and Power in the Contemporary World](https://doi.org/10.1525/9780520940635)**<br>
  Nordstrom, Carolyn. 2007. *Global Outlaws: Crime, Money, and Power in the Contemporary World*. University of California Press. ISBN 9780520250963. <https://doi.org/10.1525/9780520940635>.<br>
  **Why read it:** An anthropologist follows smuggled goods and money across borders, from African ports to European markets, and shows how illegal trade shapes economics and politics along the way. It is fieldwork rather than statistics, and it reads like it. **Who it is for:** analysts who want fieldwork rather than statistics.

*Last checked 2026-10-13.*

## Geopolitics and strategy

The strategic background for the places and conflicts that keep turning up in our work.

- <a id="hopkirk2006"></a>**[The Great Game: On Secret Service in High Asia](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9780719564475)**<br>
  Hopkirk, Peter. (1990) 2006. *The Great Game: On Secret Service in High Asia*. John Murray. ISBN 9780719564475.<br>
  **Why read it:** The nineteenth-century contest between Britain and Russia for Central Asia, told through the officers and explorers who fought it. It is the background to borders, grievances and trade routes that are still live, and it is a good story. **Who it is for:** anyone working on Central Asia, or who likes history told as a thriller.
- <a id="mcfate2020"></a>**[The New Rules of War: How America Can Win Against Russia, China, and Other Threats](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9780062843593)**<br>
  McFate, Sean. (2019) 2020. *The New Rules of War: How America Can Win Against Russia, China, and Other Threats*. William Morrow. ISBN 9780062843593.<br>
  **Why read it:** How war has changed in the twenty-first century and why conventional doctrine keeps losing to it. McFate's "durable disorder", in which states, mercenaries and criminal networks blur together, is the environment much illicit trade work sits in. **Who it is for:** security professionals thinking about conflict below the threshold of war.

*Last checked 2026-10-13.*

## OSINT and digital investigation

Method books for finding and checking information in the open. Method moves fast, so we cite the latest edition and review these every year.

- <a id="bazzell2024"></a>**[OSINT Techniques: Resources for Uncovering Online Information](https://inteltechniques.com/book1.html)**<br>
  Bazzell, Michael. 2024. *OSINT Techniques: Resources for Uncovering Online Information*. 11th ed. IntelTechniques. <https://inteltechniques.com/book1.html>. Self-published. Latest edition recommended; we review it each year.<br>
  **Why read it:** The standard practical reference for finding and analysing publicly available information: search methods, social media, virtual machines, custom tools, documentation and ethics. Bazzell rewrites it every year or two, so buy the current edition and treat older ones as history. **Who it is for:** anyone starting out in OSINT, and practitioners who want a current desk reference.

*Last checked 2026-10-13.*

## Reports and papers

Reports and papers we hand to people when a book is too slow. Each has a DOI or a stable address, and most are free to read.

- <a id="c4ads2025"></a>**[Oil and Water](https://c4ads.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/OilWater-C4ADS.pdf)**<br>
  C4ADS. 2025. *Oil and Water*. C4ADS. <https://c4ads.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/OilWater-C4ADS.pdf>. Open access.<br>
  **Why read it:** An investigation into the largest known dark fleet run by a single network: at least 56 supertankers carrying Iranian, Venezuelan and later Russian crude to China, using dark voyages, ship-to-ship transfers and GNSS spoofing to hide the oil's origin. A model of how to build a case from AIS, imagery and corporate records. **Who it is for:** analysts tracking the shadow fleet.
- <a id="allianz2026"></a>**[Safety and Shipping Review 2026](https://commercial.allianz.com/news-and-insights/reports/shipping-safety.html)**<br>
  Allianz Commercial. 2026. *Safety and Shipping Review 2026*. Allianz Commercial. <https://commercial.allianz.com/news-and-insights/reports/shipping-safety.html>. Open access.<br>
  **Why read it:** The annual review of ship losses, casualties and emerging risks from one of the largest marine insurers. It is where the shadow fleet, GNSS interference and port congestion show up as insurance numbers. **Who it is for:** anyone who needs the annual picture of what goes wrong at sea.
- <a id="europol2025"></a>**[European Union Serious and Organised Crime Threat Assessment 2025: The changing DNA of serious and organised crime](https://europol.europa.eu/publication-events/main-reports/changing-dna-of-serious-and-organised-crime)**<br>
  Europol. 2025. *European Union Serious and Organised Crime Threat Assessment 2025: The changing DNA of serious and organised crime*. Publications Office of the European Union. <https://europol.europa.eu/publication-events/main-reports/changing-dna-of-serious-and-organised-crime>. Open access.<br>
  **Why read it:** Europol's four-yearly assessment of organised crime in the EU: the criminal markets, how networks are organised and what is changing, with a strong section on logistics and ports. The reference point for European threat pictures until the next edition. **Who it is for:** anyone who needs the European picture of organised crime in one document.
- <a id="gitoc2025"></a>**[Global Organized Crime Index 2025](https://ocindex.net/)**<br>
  Global Initiative Against Transnational Organized Crime. 2025. *Global Organized Crime Index 2025*. Global Initiative Against Transnational Organized Crime. <https://ocindex.net/>. Open access.<br>
  **Why read it:** Scores for 193 countries on criminal markets, criminal actors and resilience, with the methodology published and the data downloadable. The fastest way to get oriented on a country's illicit economy before you read the detail. **Who it is for:** analysts comparing countries or briefing on a new region.
- <a id="hutten2025"></a>**[Maritime Activities Observed Through Open-Access Positioning Data: Moving and Stationary Vessels in the Baltic Sea](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.23016)**<br>
  Hütten, Moritz. 2025. *Maritime Activities Observed Through Open-Access Positioning Data: Moving and Stationary Vessels in the Baltic Sea*. arXiv. <https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2511.23016>. Open access.<br>
  **Why read it:** A study that rebuilt Baltic Sea vessel activity from open AIS data alone and came within about 20 percent of results from proprietary data. It is the best evidence we have for when the free sources are enough. **Who it is for:** analysts deciding whether free AIS data is good enough for a question.
- <a id="ohchr2022"></a>**[Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations: A Practical Guide on the Effective Use of Digital Open Source Information in Investigating Violations of International Criminal, Human Rights and Humanitarian Law](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/2024-01/OHCHR_BerkeleyProtocol.pdf)**<br>
  Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights and Human Rights Center, UC Berkeley School of Law. (2020) 2022. *Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations: A Practical Guide on the Effective Use of Digital Open Source Information in Investigating Violations of International Criminal, Human Rights and Humanitarian Law*. United Nations. <https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/2024-01/OHCHR_BerkeleyProtocol.pdf>. Open access.<br>
  **Why read it:** The standard for open source investigations that need to stand up to legal scrutiny: principles, methods, preservation and verification, and the ethics of handling material. If you read one methodology document, read this one. **Who it is for:** investigators whose findings may end up in a courtroom.
- <a id="ogundoyin2025"></a>**[Unsafe by Design?: A First Look at Security and Privacy Risks in OpenAI's Custom GPT Ecosystem](https://doi.org/10.1145/3733802.3764054)**<br>
  Ogundoyin, Sunday Oyinlola, Muhammad Ikram, Hassan Jameel Asghar, Benjamin Zi Hao Zhao, and Dali Kaafar. 2025. “Unsafe by Design?: A First Look at Security and Privacy Risks in OpenAI's Custom GPT Ecosystem.” *Proceedings of the 24th Workshop on Privacy in the Electronic Society (WPES '25)*: 147-161. <https://doi.org/10.1145/3733802.3764054>. Open access.<br>
  **Why read it:** An analysis of 14,904 custom GPTs that found most lacked basic protections: system prompts leak, roleplay and phishing attacks succeed, and the marketplace does little about it. Read it before you trust a third-party GPT with case material. The arXiv version is free to read. **Who it is for:** anyone who uses or builds custom GPTs for analysis.
- <a id="rusi2025"></a>**[RUSI Maritime Sanctions Taskforce: Insurance and Flag States](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/conference-reports/rusi-maritime-sanctions-taskforce-insurance-and-flag-states)**<br>
  Royal United Services Institute, Centre for Finance and Security. 2025. *RUSI Maritime Sanctions Taskforce: Insurance and Flag States*. RUSI. <https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/conference-reports/rusi-maritime-sanctions-taskforce-insurance-and-flag-states>. Open access.<br>
  **Why read it:** Findings from the taskforce's May 2025 meeting of about sixty government and industry members on Russia's shadow fleet: what insurers can do, how to hold flag states to account, and how to target the enablers. Short, and written for people who have to act on it. **Who it is for:** policy and compliance readers working on sanctions enforcement.
- <a id="unaishandbook2020"></a>**[AIS Handbook](https://unstats.un.org/wiki/spaces/AIS/overview)**<br>
  UN Committee of Experts on Big Data and Data Science for Official Statistics, AIS Task Team. 2020. *AIS Handbook*. United Nations Statistics Division. <https://unstats.un.org/wiki/spaces/AIS/overview>. Open access.<br>
  **Why read it:** A method handbook for working with AIS data: what the messages contain, how to clean them, how to derive port calls and voyages, and the pitfalls. Written for statisticians, which is why it is careful about what the data can and cannot show. **Who it is for:** anyone cleaning or analysing AIS data for the first time.

*Last checked 2026-10-13.*

## Wider reading

Books that are not about illicit trade but that we keep recommending anyway, because of what they teach about places, people or power.

- <a id="mathews2011"></a>**[Ghetto at the Center of the World: Chungking Mansions, Hong Kong](https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226510217.001.0001)**<br>
  Mathews, Gordon. 2011. *Ghetto at the Center of the World: Chungking Mansions, Hong Kong*. University of Chicago Press. ISBN 9780226510200. <https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226510217.001.0001>.<br>
  **Why read it:** An ethnography of one building in Hong Kong where traders from South Asia and Africa buy phones and clothes to carry home. It is the best short account we know of low-end globalisation, the informal trade that moves goods across the Global South. **Who it is for:** anyone interested in how low-end globalisation actually works.
- <a id="hao2025"></a>**[Empire of AI: Dreams and Nightmares in Sam Altman's OpenAI](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9780593657508)**<br>
  Hao, Karen. 2025. *Empire of AI: Dreams and Nightmares in Sam Altman's OpenAI*. Penguin Press. ISBN 9780593657508.<br>
  **Why read it:** A reporter's account of OpenAI and the race for AI dominance, including the labour in the Global South and the environmental cost behind the products. Useful background for anyone using these tools in intelligence work, and a reminder of where the power sits. **Who it is for:** anyone using AI tools at work who wants to know who built them and at what cost.
- <a id="kapuscinski2002"></a>**[The Shadow of the Sun](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9780679779070)**<br>
  Kapuściński, Ryszard. (1998) 2002. *The Shadow of the Sun*. Translated by Klara Glowczewska. Vintage. ISBN 9780679779070.<br>
  **Why read it:** Forty years of reporting from across Africa, from independence to the 1990s, by a Polish correspondent who travelled poor and listened. It teaches how to see a place before you analyse it. **Who it is for:** anyone heading to Africa for work for the first time.
- <a id="kaussler2020"></a>**[Rhetoric and Governance under Trump: Proclamations from the Bullshit Pulpit](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9781498594851)**<br>
  Kaussler, Bernd, Lars J. Kristiansen, and Jeffrey Delbert. 2020. *Rhetoric and Governance under Trump: Proclamations from the Bullshit Pulpit*. Lexington Books. ISBN 9781498594851.<br>
  **Why read it:** An academic study of how rhetoric shaped governance during the first Trump administration, and what it did to institutions. We include it because the language of power is part of the environment analysts report on. **Who it is for:** readers interested in how political language shapes institutions.

*Last checked 2026-10-13.*

## Contributing

Know a book, report or paper that belongs here? Please tell us. The easiest route is the [issue form](https://github.com/atlas-bear/recommended-reading/issues/new/choose), which needs no YAML. If you prefer a pull request, add a block to `data/library.yml` in the format below. We reply to every proposal, whether or not we add the title.

### What earns a place

A title goes on the list when:

1. It helps someone work on illicit trade, or on the economics, politics, places and methods around it. The "Wider reading" section exists for the second kind.
2. Someone at Atlas Bear has read it. We do not list on reputation.
3. It can be obtained: in print, through a library or publisher subscription, or free online. Out-of-print titles stay only when there is no replacement, and are marked.
4. It has a full citation: authors in published order, publisher, year, and an ISBN-13 for the edition we recommend, or a DOI or stable address for papers and reports.
5. It adds something the list does not already have. Two books on the same subject need a reason.

Authors and publishers are welcome to propose their own work if they say so. We treat those proposals exactly like any other and read the work before deciding.

### The entry format

Every title is one block in `data/library.yml`. The fields are explained in the comment at the top of that file. A book looks like this:

```yaml
  - id: shelley2020
    type: book
    title: Dark Commerce
    subtitle: How a New Illicit Economy Is Threatening Our Future
    authors:
      - {family: Shelley, given: Louise I.}
    original_year: 2018
    publisher: Princeton University Press
    place: Princeton
    year: 2020
    isbn13: "9780691209760"
    doi: "10.1515/9780691184296"
    openlibrary: OL20186908W
    access: print
    section: financial-crime
    audience: anyone who needs the big picture of illicit trade in one book
    why: >
      Two or three plain sentences on what the book does and why it matters for this work.
    added: 2025-07-12
    last_checked: 2026-10-13
```

A few rules keep the list consistent:

- **Cite the edition you recommend.** The ISBN-13 is the one printed in that edition; the year is that edition's year, with `original_year` for the first publication if it differs. For method books that are rewritten often, set `rolling: true` and we will cite whatever edition is current.
- **Organisations are authors too.** Write `{literal: "Europol"}` for a corporate author.
- **Reports and papers** need a DOI where one exists, otherwise the publisher's stable address, and `access: open` if anyone can read them for free.
- **The "why" is plain.** Two or three sentences in ordinary words about what the reader gets, tied to illicit trade work. No marketing language and no em dashes. **The "audience"** is one short line.
- **No retailer links.** Titles link to Open Library or to the DOI. We do not use affiliate links, prices or ratings.

### Generated files

`README.md`, the list pages in `docs/`, and the files in `exports/` are built from `data/library.yml` by `scripts/build.py`. Please do not edit them directly; the check on every pull request will fail if they differ from the YAML. If you can run Python, run the script and commit everything it changes. If not, tick "Allow edits by maintainers" and we will regenerate on your branch.

```bash
pip install pyyaml
python scripts/build.py          # rebuild README, docs and exports
python scripts/build.py --check  # what CI runs
python scripts/verify.py         # check ISBNs and DOIs against the registries (network)
```

The build checks every ISBN's check digit, the shape of every DOI, duplicate identifiers and the required fields. Once a month a workflow checks each ISBN against Open Library and each DOI against Crossref or DataCite, and opens an issue if something no longer resolves.

### Reviews and releases

We sweep the whole list twice a year with our other lists, in spring before the [Summer School on Illicit Trade](https://www.rug.nl/education/summer-winter-schools/illicit-trade) and again in autumn: editions, availability and whether each title still earns its place. Each sweep ends with a release and a [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md) entry. Please keep to our [community guidelines](COMMUNITY_GUIDELINES.md) in issues and pull requests.
